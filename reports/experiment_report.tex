\topic{How to read the results}
Weight class errors equally. Score $f(x)\in[-1,1]$ gives probability $p_0(x)=(1+f(x))/2$ of accepting class zero. The primary metric is

\[\mathrm{BER}_{\rm prob}=\tfrac12\left[\operatorname{mean}_{Y=0}(1-p_0(X))+\operatorname{mean}_{Y=1}p_0(X)\right].\]
Lower error is better; $50\%$ is the no-information level of a constant prediction. We also report error from classifying by the score's sign. In synthetic data, selecting $h$ true signals gives precision $h/d$ and recall $h/s$; high recall alone does not establish economical selection.

\topic{How data are generated and split}
Each row is a record labelled $k=0,1$, and each column is a numerical variable. $D$ is the candidate count, $n$ the training count per class, $s$ the true signal count, and $d$ the allowed selected count. For example, $D=50,n=20,s=4$ gives forty training rows and fifty columns: four signals and forty-six noise variables.

Generate each synthetic row's $s$ signals using the rules below, then append $D-s$ independent standard normal noise variables. Draw every $Z_j$ independently across rows and coordinates; in the mixture model, draw the row-specific sign $B=\pm1$ with equal probability. Randomly permute columns in each repetition, sharing that permutation across splits and methods.

The main experiment uses $s=4$ at three scales, $(D,n)=(20,10),(50,20),(100,50)$, each with $d=2,4,8$. At the medium scale also use $s=2$ with $d=1,2,4$, and $s=8$ with $d=4,8,16$. \topic{Which methods are compared, and how is the comparison kept fair?}
Separate the selection criterion from the search procedure. The first four procedures below optimize the same robust risk; the remaining five provide different selection criteria. Every method selects variables using training data and refits the same robust score at the same budget.

{\small\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}P{32mm}X@{}}\toprule
Method & What it does and why it is included\\\midrule
Enumeration & Fit every equal-budget subset; used only on small problems to obtain an optimal-value reference.\\
Global search & Our algorithm. Use risk lower bounds from evaluated subsets to reduce the search, returning a feasible solution and the remaining possible improvement.\\
Forward / forward with swaps & Add the variable yielding the lowest risk at each step; the latter then tries one-for-one improvements. Test whether a more local search is sufficient.\\
Marginal MMD & Rank columns by their class-distribution difference. MMD (maximum mean discrepancy) compares within-class and between-class similarities, detecting more than a change in the mean.\\
L1 logistic regression & Fit a penalized linear classifier and select the largest absolute coefficients, representing standard sparse linear selection.\\
mRMR & Prefer label-related variables without duplicating selected information; measure dependence by mutual information after binning at training tertiles.\\
Joint MMD & Compare class distributions on a group of variables, using forward steps and swaps to increase the difference; a joint criterion that can use dependencies.\\
Uniform random & Draw an equal-size subset with a saved seed, providing an uninformed selection reference.\\
\bottomrule\end{tabularx}}
Both MMD methods use Gaussian similarity with bandwidth equal to the median positive training distance on the current subset. Their score adds the two within-class mean similarities and subtracts twice the between-class mean, excluding within-class self-pairs. Joint MMD and robust forward with swaps accept at most ten improvements. All true signals and all variables are interpretive references, excluded from equal-budget rankings.

\topic{Parameter selection never accesses test data}
Standardize using pooled training means and standard deviations only, dropping training-constant columns. The robust radius sets the allowed distribution movement. Its scale is the classwise median pairwise sum of absolute training differences divided by retained dimension. Compare multipliers $1/8,1/4,1/2,1,2$; at reference budget four (sixteen for HAR), select one common radius by mean validation error from marginal MMD, mRMR and joint MMD. Fix it for every method and budget. Validate L1 parameters among $0.01,0.1,1,10,100$; ties favor stronger regularization, a larger radius and a smaller column index.

Summarize synthetic results over thirty independent datasets. Compute method differences within each dataset, then use two thousand paired bootstrap resamples for $95\%$ intervals. The ten tabular splits measure split sensitivity, rather than independent test populations. For HAR, average training repetitions within test person, then equally weight and resample the nine people; windows and repetitions do not become new people.

\topic{Current conclusions and limits}
Current evidence supports agreement between small-scale optimization and the independent reference, while exposing a substantial all-variable prediction problem. Selection gains, performance under distribution change and larger-scale efficiency each require their corresponding completed experiment.

Observed results are separated from the plan still being executed. Small-problem correctness is not statistical generalization, and the specified shifts do not cover every real change. Future score or algorithm changes require a separately identified code version and independent validation data.
